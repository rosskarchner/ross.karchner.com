[About a month ago](https://ross.karchner.com/2023/10/21/i-took-the.html), I migrated my mastodon account over to this blog (via micro.blog's built-in support for ActivityPub). How's that going?

Mostly good. I like that I've got a full-fledged personal website here, that is fully customizable and could theoretically run anywhere. Behind the scenes, it's just [hugo](https://gohugo.io/).

I appreciate how micro.blog is trying to differentiate itself from [traditional social media](https://help.micro.blog/t/whats-the-difference-between-micro-blog-and-mastodon/1395), creating a space that doesn't reward popularity or sensationalism. There are no trending topics, follower counts, likes, or retweets to chase-- just people writing and the things they write.

Of course, much of this is "good like eating your vegetables" and not "good like eating a cheeseburger". All those little dopamine hits that make things like Twitter, Facebook, and Mastodon addictive and un-calm are also what make them fun.

I think discovery is worse here. [The emoji thing is cute](https://help.micro.blog/t/emoji-in-discover/34) but I don't think it works well in practice. For example, m.b wants 🖋️  to signify "pens and ink", [but sometimes it doesn't](https://tiv.today/2023/09/writersstrikedeal). It'd also be nice to not have a hard-coded set of topics. Really, I just want hashtags, and I want to be able to follow hashtags. Like Mastodon. I can see how "trending topics" is a feature to avoid, but it's perfectly possible to have hashtags without elevating trends.

One thing I *have* found useful is the [book covers page](https://micro.blog/discover/books/grid). If you see someone posting about a book you like or that sounds interesting to you, that's probably a good person to follow.
